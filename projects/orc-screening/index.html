Physics-constrained screening across chemical space

Combining ML property prediction with equation-of-state calculations and engineering constraints to narrow a very large candidate space.

Question

How do you get from a huge space of possible working fluids to a short list engineers can review?

Approach

ML-predicted properties feed equation-of-state and cycle calculations, and engineering constraints filter the results.

Result

About 800 million molecules narrowed to 19 candidates for engineering review.

My role

Designed and built the screening workflow, linking the ML models to the physics calculations.

Each stage is cheaper than the next, so expensive physics runs only on candidates that pass the earlier filters.

Why ML alone was not enough

A working fluid has to satisfy several physical and operating requirements at once. Brute-force physics on hundreds of millions of molecules is impractical, while ML predictions alone can rank molecules highly that are physically implausible. So ML handled the scale, and equation-of-state calculations and operating constraints made sure the survivors made physical sense.

The candidates are working fluids for an organic Rankine cycle, so their properties are judged by how the cycle performs.

Limitations

  • Errors in the ML-predicted critical properties carry through to the cycle calculations, so the shortlist is a prioritization for expert review, not a final selection.
  • Safety, toxicity, and cost need checks beyond this workflow.

Takeaway

Scientific ML is most useful inside a physics-aware workflow, judged by the quality of the decisions it supports.
