Physics-constrained screening across chemical space

Combining ML property prediction with equation-of-state calculations and engineering constraints to reduce a massive candidate space.

This project asked how to move from a huge space of possible molecules to a short list that engineers could realistically review. The key idea was that ML was one component of a larger decision system, not the full solution by itself.

Figure 1. A screening funnel from a very large chemical design space to a small engineering review set.

The scientific problem

Candidate working fluids must satisfy multiple physical and operational requirements. Brute-force evaluation across an enormous design space is not practical, so the challenge was to combine prediction, thermodynamic modeling, and feasibility checks in a coherent pipeline.

Approach

I built a workflow that used ML-predicted thermophysical properties as inputs to downstream physics calculations. Equation-of-state calculations and operating constraints filtered the search further, ensuring that high-scoring molecules also remained plausible under engineering considerations.

Why this matters

The value of the project was not only the scale. It demonstrated how scientific ML becomes more useful when it is embedded inside a physics-aware workflow and evaluated according to the quality of the final decisions it enables.
