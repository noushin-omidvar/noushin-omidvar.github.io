Learning to rank electrolyte formulations by cycle life

Ranking electrolyte formulations for experimental prioritization, with features built from component chemistry and validation on held-out component combinations.

Question

Cycling experiments are slow and expensive. Which formulations should the lab test first?

Approach

Formulation features aggregated from component chemistry, with a tree-based learning-to-rank model.

Result

NDCG@10 ≈ 0.95 and Spearman ≈ 0.66 on held-out component combinations.

My role

Problem framing, featurization, modeling, and validation design.

Why ranking, not regression

The lab's decision was not "what exact cycle life will this cell reach?" but "which candidates deserve the next experiments?" That made learning to rank the natural objective, and ranking metrics the natural way to judge it: NDCG@10 asks whether the best formulations reach the top of the list, and Spearman asks whether the overall order is meaningful.

Representing a mixture

An electrolyte is a mixture of solvents, salts, and additives. I computed molecular and DFT-derived descriptors for each component and aggregated them into fixed-length formulation features using composition-weighted means, minima, maxima, and ranges.

Validation

The 1,424 experiments include replicate cells of the same formulation, and formulations that share components are not independent, so a random split would leak information. I used nested grouped cross-validation that held out whole component combinations: the model was always scored on formulations whose component set it had not seen during training or tuning.

Limitations

  • 158 formulations is a small set, so the ranking is most reliable within the chemistry the campaign covered.
  • Aggregate mixture features cannot represent specific component–component interactions.

Takeaway

The downstream decision should set the objective and the metric. Here that meant ranking, and validation that respects how mixtures share components.
