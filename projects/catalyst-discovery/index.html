Physics-informed graph learning for catalyst discovery

A theory-infused graph neural network, active learning with DFT, and explainable AI to screen Pt-monolayer core–shell catalysts for oxygen reduction and explain their reactivity.

Question

Can a model search a large catalyst space and also explain why one Pt surface binds *OH differently from another?

Approach

TinNet (a GNN with Newns–Anderson chemisorption theory built in), active learning with DFT, and SHAP on its physical variables.

Result

The screen recovered known active catalysts and proposed new core–shell candidates; cost filtering favored cores containing earth-abundant Fe, Co, and Ni. Reactivity depends on more than the d-band center.

My role

First author. Built the core–shell candidate space, ran the active-learning loop with DFT, and led the explainability analysis. TinNet itself comes from Wang et al., Nat. Commun. 2021.

Core–shell structures are represented as graphs. TinNet links the learned representation to chemisorption theory, and SHAP links changes in *OH binding to electronic factors.

Why build theory into the model

Pt binds oxygen intermediates of the oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) slightly too strongly, so the target was *OH binding 0–0.15 eV weaker than on Pt(111). A standard GNN can predict binding energies, but its internal features have no physical meaning. TinNet instead predicts electronic quantities, d-band moments and adsorbate orbital parameters, and passes them through the Newns–Anderson model. That makes the explanations interpretable in surface-science terms.

Searching with active learning

Each round, DFT was run only where the model was most uncertain or most promising.

Rather than running DFT on every candidate, each round added calculations where new data was most informative, and prediction uncertainty fell over the rounds. Against DFT, nested 10-fold cross-validation gave an MAE of about 0.05 eV (standard deviation about 0.04 eV). That is small next to the typical error of GGA-level DFT itself relative to experiment (roughly 0.1–0.2 eV for adsorption energies), so the model adds little error beyond the method it learns from.

What the model explained

SHAP on TinNet's physical variables showed that the d-band center alone does not explain the trends. The renormalized OH 1π resonance energy and its coupling to the metal d-states also matter. For Pt₃Co@PtML, which binds *OH about 0.13 eV more weakly than Pt(111), the analysis traces most of the change to compressive strain from the core, which shifts the Pt d-band center down and changes the 1π–d coupling.

Limitations

  • *OH binding is a single activity descriptor; it does not capture stability under reaction conditions, such as dissolution or segregation.
  • The new candidates are computational predictions and still need synthesis and electrochemical testing.
